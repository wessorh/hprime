\begin{abstract}
After 11 Hilbert orders, 6 fundamental curve constructions, 2 independent
operator formulations, 4D comparative analysis, 30,000 permutation tests,
and exhaustive optimization over the 3D Hilbert construction space, we
conclude that the 3D Hilbert plane operator cannot prove the Riemann
Hypothesis.  The eigenvalue spectrum correlates with zeta zeros at
$r \approx -0.80$, but the eigenvalues are not the zeros.  The gap
between correlation and identity cannot be closed at computationally
accessible orders, and there is no evidence that it closes in the limit.
This paper explains exactly why, and what would be required for any
geometric approach to succeed where this one did not.
\end{abstract}

\section{What We Found}

The investigation established three robust phenomena:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Plane-density anomaly:} Primes are not uniformly distributed
   across Z-planes of the 3D Hilbert curve.  The density variation grows
   as $2^{n/2}$, reaching $|z| = 55.7$ at order 10 ($p < 10^{-200}$).

\item \textbf{PNT error alignment:} 98\% of planes with anomalous prime
   density sample integer ranges where $\pi(x) > \text{li}(x)$ -- the
   explicit formula's oscillatory term is systematically positive.

\item \textbf{Spectral correlation:} Eigenvalues of the Hilbert plane
   covariance operator correlate with zeta zeros at $r \approx -0.80$
   across six orders and two operator constructions ($p < 10^{-4}$,
   30,000 permutation tests).
\end{enumerate}

These are genuine mathematical discoveries.  The 3D Hilbert curve encodes
structural information about prime distribution that is statistically
indistinguishable from the zeta function's spectral content at the
correlation level.

\section{Why This Cannot Prove RH}

Despite the strength of the correlations, five fundamental gaps prevent
this approach from constituting a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.

\subsection{1. Correlation Is Not Identity}

The eigenvalues of the Hilbert plane operator \emph{correlate with} the
zeta zeros at $r \approx -0.80$.  They are not \emph{equal to} the zeta
zeros.  The mean absolute deviation between the normalized eigenvalue
spectrum and the normalized zeta zero spectrum is stable at $\approx 0.28$
across orders 6--11, showing no statistically significant convergence
toward zero.

A correlation of $r = -0.80$ means the eigenvalues are in approximately
the correct \emph{order}, but their precise values differ from the zeta
zeros by $\sim 28\%$ of the spectral range.  For the Hilbert-P\'{o}lya
conjecture to be realized, the eigenvalues must \emph{equal} the zeta
zeros exactly, not merely correlate with them.

The temperature in London correlates with the temperature in New York at
$r \approx 0.7$, but that does not make London's thermometer a measurement
of New York's weather.  Correlation, no matter how statistically
significant, does not establish identity.

\subsection{2. Wrong Spectral Universality Class}

The eigenvalue pair correlation function is anti-GUE ($r_{\text{GUE}} =
-0.84$): the eigenvalues exhibit \emph{clustering} rather than the
\emph{level repulsion} characteristic of zeta zeros.  Montgomery's pair
correlation conjecture -- one of the most thoroughly verified predictions
in analytic number theory -- states that zeta zero spacings follow the
GUE distribution.  The Hilbert plane operator's eigenvalues follow the
opposite pattern.

This is not a minor discrepancy.  The GUE property of zeta zeros is
believed to be a universal signature of quantum chaotic systems.  An
operator whose spectrum is anti-GUE belongs to a fundamentally different
dynamical class -- an integrable or quasi-integrable system rather than
a chaotic one.  If the zeta zeros are eigenvalues of a quantum chaotic
Hamiltonian (as the GUE statistics suggest), the Hilbert plane operator
cannot be that Hamiltonian.

\subsection{3. No Convergence in Accessible Regime}

The scaling analysis (Section 5.3 of Paper IV) shows that the eigenvalue
error relative to zeta zeros converges at a rate of approximately $-0.07$
per order.  At this rate, order $\approx 475$ would be required for 1\%
accuracy -- a matrix of size $2^{475} \times 2^{475}$, requiring more
memory than there are atoms in the observable universe.

The physical universe imposes a hard limit on computational verification.
If convergence to the zeta zeros requires orders that cannot be realized
in this universe, then the conjecture cannot be verified computationally.
And if it cannot be verified, it cannot serve as the basis for a proof
that relies on computational evidence.

\subsection{4. The Operator Is Not the Right One}

The Hilbert plane operator $T_n$ is a covariance matrix constructed from
adjacent integer pairs along the Hilbert curve.  It measures
\emph{correlations between planes}, not the \emph{action of an operator
on arithmetic functions}.  The true Hilbert--P\'{o}lya operator $\hat{H}$
would satisfy:

\begin{equation}
\hat{H} \phi_k = \gamma_k \phi_k
\end{equation}

where $\phi_k$ are eigenfunctions and $\gamma_k$ are the zeta zeros.
The Hilbert plane operator does not have this property for any known
set of $\phi_k$.  Its eigenvalues are the wrong values (MAD $\approx
0.28$), and its eigenvectors are the wrong objects (plane indicator
functions rather than arithmetic wavefunctions).

The fact that the eigenvalues correlate with the zeta zeros means the
operator is \emph{related to} $\hat{H}$, not that it \emph{is} $\hat{H}$.

\subsection{5. The Gap Is Analytic, Not Computational}

The most fundamental obstacle is that the zeta zeros are defined
analytically -- as the roots of a complex function continued to the
entire complex plane -- while the Hilbert plane operator is defined
combinatorially -- as a finite-dimensional matrix built from a
discrete geometric construction.

Bridging this gap requires showing that the combinatorial construction
converges to the analytic one in the limit $n \to \infty$.  This is
an analytic continuation problem, not a computational one.  No amount
of computational evidence at finite $n$ can substitute for a proof of
convergence in the limit.

The situation is analogous to approximating $\pi$ by the perimeter of
an inscribed $2^n$-gon.  At $n=10$, the approximation is good to several
decimal places.  But you cannot \emph{prove} that the limit is $\pi$
without an analytic argument -- the computational evidence is suggestive
but not dispositive.

\section{What Would Be Required}

For any geometric construction to prove the Riemann Hypothesis via the
Hilbert--P\'{o}lya pathway, it must satisfy four criteria:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact eigenvalue match:} The eigenvalues must equal the
   zeta zeros, not merely correlate with them.  MAD must converge to 0.

\item \textbf{GUE spacing statistics:} The eigenvalue pair correlation
   function must match GUE at all accessible orders, not anti-GUE.

\item \textbf{Proven self-adjointness:} The infinite-order limit must be
   proven self-adjoint by analytic argument, not computational evidence.

\item \textbf{Analytic convergence proof:} The finite-order approximations
   must be proven to converge to the limit, with error bounds that vanish
   as $n \to \infty$.
\end{enumerate}

The 3D Hilbert plane operator satisfies none of these criteria.  It
achieves the first criterion partially (correlation but not identity),
fails the second (anti-GUE), and has no proof for the third or fourth.

\section{What This Work Contributes Despite Not Proving RH}

The investigation was not a failure.  It established:

\begin{itemize}
\item The first computationally accessible operator whose spectrum
   correlates with zeta zeros at statistically overwhelming significance.

\item A new geometric probe of prime distribution -- the 3D Hilbert
   curve's plane decomposition -- that reveals structural biases
   invisible to other methods.

\item The specificity of the 3D case: the correlation is unique to 3D
   (4D weakens with order) and bounded across curve constructions,
   suggesting a genuine mathematical structure rather than an artifact.

\item A clear negative result: exhaustive optimization over the 3D
   Hilbert construction space cannot close the gap to identity.

\item A set of criteria that any future geometric approach must satisfy
   to succeed where this one fell short.
\end{itemize}

In science, knowing what doesn't work is often as valuable as knowing
what does.  The 3D Hilbert curve will not prove the Riemann Hypothesis.
But the tools, methods, and structural insights developed here may
enable someone else to find the construction that will.
